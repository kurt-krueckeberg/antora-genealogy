[IMPORTANT]
====
We know that in September of 1808 Jobst Heinrich Krückeberg was living as
an Einlieger with the Wilharms of no. 11 Evesen.

We know this because in September of 1808, Jobst Heinrich petitioned the
Rentkammer for a plot of land for house construction near Bärenbusch. In the first
of the case file xref:nla:146:index.adoc[] documents, 
xref:nla:146:doc1.adoc[], dated 29 September 1808, Jobst Heinrich
Krückeberg states: "Up to now I have lived as a tenant with Wilharm No. 11
in Evesen".

We know that this Wilharm was Hans Heinrich from his
xref:petzen:PET-M-1796a.adoc[1796 marriage to Anne Marie Philippine Blecke]
of no. 11 Evesen. We know no. 11 Evesen is were the coupled resided from
the xref:petzen:PET-B-1798c.adoc[birth record of their daugther Anne Marie
Christine] and that of their son xref:petzen:PET-B-1810a.adoc[Carl
Heinrich].
====

.Meaning of Einlieger
****
[NOTE]
====
*“Schneider und Einlieger in Nr. 14 in Bärenbusch”* tells us two separate
things: his trade (tailor) and his social/tenurial position (Einlieger). An
Einlieger in early-19th-century rural Germany was typically a lodger or
subtenant without a full farm or house place of their own—part of the
unterbäuerliche (sub-peasant) strata who lived “in” an existing holding rather
than as the recognized holder of it. 

So “Einlieger in Nr. 14” very strongly suggests that Carl Friedrich Gottlieb
Krückeberg did not own or inherit farm no. 14, but instead rented rooms or a
small secondary dwelling on that property, supporting himself as a tailor while
living in the household (or on the land) of whoever was the actual
Colon/householder of No. 14 in Berenbusch.
====
****
