.Translation
[literal.bordered,subs="verbatim,quotes"]
....
In the year 1731,
on the 3rd of January, Henrich Vahlsing, formerly a shepherd,
32 years of age, from the high-comital Vorwerk of Höckersau,
was buried, being 63 years old.
....

=== Explanation of Vorwerk of Höckersau

In the early-eighteenth-century territorial structure of Schaumburg-Lippe,
a *Vorwerk* was an outlying agricultural estate that formed part of a
larger lordly domain rather than an independent village holding. Such
estates were owned directly by the territorial lord, in this case the Count
of Schaumburg-Lippe, which is why the record describes it as *hochgräflich*
(“high-comital”). A Vorwerk functioned as a productive extension of the
comital economy, typically devoted to grain cultivation, sheep husbandry,
or other estate-managed agriculture, and its output was intended to supply
the lord’s household, administration, or revenues rather than to support a
hereditary peasant family.

The designation “Vorwerk of Höckersau” therefore does not identify a free
village or a numbered peasant holding but names a specific estate unit
administered as part of the comital lands. People associated with a
Vorwerk, such as shepherds, farmhands, or overseers, lived and worked there
by virtue of employment or assignment, not hereditary tenure. Their
residence was administrative rather than civic, and they would not appear
in land registers as holders of a *Stätte*, *Bauerngut*, or *Colonie*. In
this burial entry, the phrase indicates that Henrich Vahlsing belonged to
the workforce of the comital estate at Höckersau and that his social and
legal position was tied to comital service rather than to ownership or
tenancy of peasant land.

Translated into English, “high-comital Vorwerk of Höckersau” is therefore
best understood as “the outlying estate of Höckersau belonging to the
count,” with *Vorwerk* left untranslated to preserve its specific
administrative meaning.
