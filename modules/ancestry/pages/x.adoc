Yes—**once an Einlieger was granted an *erbliches Colonat* and actually established residence (house built and occupied), he became a *Colon* (often styled *Neubauer* in the establishment phase).** The decisive factor was **not merely the grant**, but **completion of settlement** and entry into the obligations attached to the holding.

Below is the status transition as it functioned in **Schaumburg-Lippe ca. 1800–1810**.

---

## 1. The decisive threshold: settlement, not approval

Approval by the Rentkammer created a **right in expectancy** (*Anwartschaft*), but **status changed only when**:

* the house was built (*Hausbau vollzogen*),
* the grantee **moved in** and took possession,
* the holding entered the **tax and dues register** as a *Colonat*.

Only then did the Einlieger **cease to be landless**.

---

## 2. Status before and after (Standeswechsel)

### **Before**

**Einlieger**

* No independent holding
* No house or only lodging with another household
* No seat in village administration
* Limited legal standing in agrarian matters
* Paid minor dues tied to residence or labor capacity

### **After**

**Colon (often initially: Neubauer)**

* Holder of an **erbliches Colonat**
* Independent household (*Hausvater*)
* Hereditary possession (subject to confirmation)
* Regular annual dues (*Erbzins*, services, fees)
* Entered in fiscal, police, and parish records as a **Bauer** (in the broad legal sense)

This is a **formal Standeswechsel**, not merely an economic improvement.

---

## 3. What *Colon* meant legally in Schaumburg-Lippe

Becoming a *Colon* entailed:

* **Agrarian-legal status**
  The Colon was a recognized rural estate-holder under cameral law.

* **Fiscal identity**
  The holding appeared in Rentkammer, Rentmeister, and Amts accounts under its own number.

* **Hereditary continuity**
  The holding passed to heirs (with lordly confirmation), unlike Einlieger arrangements.

* **Local standing**
  Eligibility for village obligations, policing, and in some cases participation in communal decisions.

Importantly, **ownership of the soil remained with the Kammer**. The Colon held a **heritable right of use**, not title.

---

## 4. Why the documents emphasize *Ausweisung* and *Hausbau*

You will often see language such as:

* *Ausweisung an den Neubauer …*
* *… sind X Quadratruthen zum Hausbau ausgewiesen worden*
* *… zum ersten male zu entrichten*

These phrases mark the **transition point** from Einlieger to Colon:

* *Ausweisung* = spatial and legal fixation of the holding
* *Hausbau* = activation of the status
* first due date = entry into the Colon’s fiscal regime

---

## 5. Terminological precision for your work

For your translations and commentary, the most accurate formulations are:

* **“Upon completion of house construction and settlement, he became a Colon (hereditary tenant-holder).”**
* **“The grant of an *erbliches Colonat* effected a formal change of status from Einlieger to Colon once the holding was occupied.”**

Avoid implying:

* freehold ownership,
* immediate status change at approval alone,
* purely contractual lease.
