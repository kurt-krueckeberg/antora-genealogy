= Non-Church Records for Schaumburg-Lippe (Berenbusch / Petzen, ca. 1725–1750)

Although no full census survives for this period, *non-Kirchenbuch* records in the
*Niedersächsisches Landesarchiv – Abteilung Bückeburg (NLA BU)* can still identify
nearly every household in *Berenbusch* or nearby villages of the Amt Bückeburg.

== 1. Key record types

[arabic]
. *Tax / Contribution Registers (Kontributions- & Schatzungsregister)*  
+
These are periodic lists of taxpayers by village and status (Colon, Anbauer, Einwohner).
They often note predecessors (“jetzt …, zuvor …”).  
+
➜ They identify which *Krückeberg heads* paid in Berenbusch and their social rank.  
+
*See Series:*
+
** *K 1--Rentkammer Bückeburg*
** *A 12--Amt Bückeburg*
** *F 2--Kontributionen und Steuern*
** *L 1 / K 90--fiscal fonds*

. *Land / Farm Registers (Lager-, Hof- or Meierbücher)*  
+
These are lease books listing each holding, acreage, dues, and tenant succession.
+  
➜ They link a named *Krückeberg* to a specific farm; later entries back-reference earlier tenants.

. *Farm Charters & Estate Files (Hof-, Kauf-, Tausch-, Übergabe-, Meier- and Altenteilbriefe; Inventare & Vormundschaftsakten)*  
+
These are contracts for transfers or retirements; probate and guardianship files.
+
➜ They identify *parents, heirs, and spouses*; inventories list *children with ages*.  
+
*See Series:*

** *K 1--Rentkammer Bückeburg*
** *A 12--Amt Bückeburg*
** *H 121d--Freiwillige Gerichtsbarkeit*.
. *Militia / Service Lists (Miliz-, Musterungs-, Dienstregister)*  
+
They enumerate eligible males by village and year.
+  
➜ They fix *Jobst* vs *Johann Heinrich Krückeberg* to specific years.
+
*See Series:*

** *F 2--Allgemeine Militärsachen*.
. *Fire-Insurance Registers (Brandkasse)*  
+
These are house-by-house valuations, often noting “jetzt …, vor diesem …”.  
+
➜ They extend continuity between the 1790s and the 1720s.
+  
*See Series:* Rentkammer or Amt Bückeburg.
. *Local Administrative / Forst / Boundary Files*
+
These are village-level disputes, services, or wood-use records.
+
➜ They would place two Krückeberg men in one context—brothers, heirs, or neighbors.

== 2. Coverage reality (1728–1750)

* There are no true censuses; only household-head lists.  
* The surviving evidence includes:  
** tax / land registers keyed to *household heads*, and  
** inheritance / guardianship files when farms changed hands.
  
Combined, they can identify all Krückeberg households active in the village.

== 4. Step-by-step Arcinsys search strategy

=== Definition of "fonds"

*Fonds* (pronounced roughly _fonz_, from French) means:

____
_The entire body of records that originate from a single creator — a
person, family, or institution — accumulated naturally as the result of
its activities and preserved as an organic whole._
____

In other words, a _fonds_ is *not* an artificial collection arranged by
subject, but the _original aggregate_ of records produced by one entity
in the course of doing its work.

=== 🏛 In plain terms

Think of a _fonds_ as:

____
"`Everything that came out of one office, kept together because it all
belonged to that office.`"
____

For example:

[width="100%",cols="21%,33%,46%",options="header",]
|===
|Fonds |Creator |What it represents
|`+K 1 Rentkammer Bückeburg+` |The _Rentkammer_ (treasury/land office)
of Schaumburg-Lippe |All land, tax, and lease files that office
generated or received

|`+A 12 Amt Bückeburg+` |The _Amt Bückeburg_ (district administration)
|All administrative, judicial, and police records created by that local
government

|`+H 121d Freiwillige Gerichtsbarkeit+` |The probate and guardianship
courts |All wills, inventories, and guardianship files they maintained
|===

Each of these is a _fonds_ because it preserves the original context of
record creation — who made the record and why.

=== 🧩 Relationship to "`series`" and "`files`"

In most archival hierarchies (including the NLA’s _Arcinsys_):

....
Archive
 ├── Fonds (Bestand)
 │     ├── Series (Serie)
 │     │     └── Files (Akte, Band, Nr.)
....

* The *fonds* = the whole record group from one office.
* A *series* = a subdivision within that fonds (for example, "`Tax rolls
1710–1800`").
* An *Akte* or *Nr.* = a specific file or bound volume within that
series.

When you search Arcinsys and see entries like
`+K 1 Nr. 842 – Berenbusch, Kontributionsregister 1743–1752+`, you’re
looking at:

* Fonds: `+K 1+` (Rentkammer Bückeburg)
* Series: "`Tax rolls`" (implied)
* File: Nr. 842 (the specific item you can request)

=== 🧭 Why it matters

Understanding _fonds_ helps you:

* Keep context — know who produced a record and why.
* Avoid mixing files from different creators (which can distort
meaning).
* Search more efficiently, since Arcinsys is organized by fonds rather
than by topic.

=== What “series for Amt Bückeburg / Rentkammer … and Untergericht Bückeburg” means

The archive organizes its holdings (*Bestände*) by the government office that originally created them.
For your period (1725–1750) the relevant offices for *Berenbusch* were:

* *Amt Bückeburg* – the local administrative and judicial district that governed Petzen, Berenbusch, Nordholz, etc.
* *Rentkammer Bückeburg* (also called *Kammer- or Domänenverwaltung*) – the treasury and land office that managed taxes, leases, and meierhof records.
* *Untergericht Bückeburg* – the lower court that handled local legal and inheritance matters.

When you look in the NLA catalogue (*Arcinsys*), you search inside the *series (Bestände)* for those offices — that’s where documents about villagers actually appear.

=== What “Ortsregister / Findbücher keywords” means

Every archival series has an index (*Findbuch* or *Ortsregister*) — basically a searchable list of files.  
When you consult staff or use *Arcinsys*, you tell them the place and subject you’re after.  
For example, you would say:

> Bitte nach Unterlagen im Bestand K 1 (Rentkammer Bückeburg) bzw. A 12 (Amt Bückeburg) mit den Stichwörtern *Berenbusch*, *Petzen* und den Sachbegriffen *Kontribution*, *Schatzung*, *Lagerbuch*, *Meier*, *Hofbrief*, *Altenteil*, *Inventar*, *Vormundschaft*, *Miliz*, *Brandkasse* suchen.

That means:

> Please look in the Rentkammer and Amt Bückeburg series for files relating to Berenbusch/Petzen that concern taxation, land leases, farm charters, inheritance contracts, guardianships, militia lists, or fire-insurance records.

|*Lagerbuch / Meierregister*

|*Hofbrief / Meierbrief / Kaufbrief*

|Identifies buyer/seller, kin, and witnesses

|*Altenteil*

|Lists parents and heirs; shows farm succession

|*Inventar / Vormundschaft*
|Probate or guardianship records
|Names of children, ages, and relationships

|*Militär / Milice*
|Militia or recruiting lists
|Adult males by village and year

|Fire-insurance registers
|Householders and building valuations
|===

These are the technical search words archivists recognize when filtering the indexes.

=== Search Strategy

[arabic]
. **Limit the fonds.**  
   Start with *Bestand = K 1 (Rentkammer Bückeburg)*.  

. **Run separate searches** combining the place and subject:  
+
----
Berenbusch Kontribution
Berenbusch Schatzung
Berenbusch Lagerbuch
Berenbusch Meier
Berenbusch Hofbrief
Berenbusch Altenteil
Berenbusch Inventar
Berenbusch Vormundschaft
Berenbusch Brandkasse
----
. **Repeat** with *Bestand = A 12 (Amt Bückeburg)* adding  
   `Miliz`, `Musterung`, `Dienst`.  

. **Check Laufzeiten.**  
   Favor files dated 1700–1750 (± 10 years). Later ones often reference earlier tenants.  

. **Record key data** for each hit:
+  
`Year • Record type • Village • Status • Name • Holding • Kin • Predecessor/Successor`.  

. **Prioritize** early *Lagerbuch*, *Schatzregister/Kontribution*, and *Brandkasse* for household lists;  
   then *Hofbrief/Altenteil/Inventar/Vormundschaft* for kinship proof.  

. **Correlate** with Petzen church entries by status and overlap (“Colon Krückeberg” vs “Anbauer Krückeberg”).

== 6. How this helps your Krückeberg research

* Determine how many *Krückeberg* households co-existed in Berenbusch.  
* Identify which held which *Kolon* (farm).  
* Trace inheritance and property succession.  
* Provide names where Petzen church books say only “Krückeberg in Berenbusch.”  
These records bridge the gap between the 1728–1750 Kirchenbücher and the later 1798–1800 case files.
