= Non-Church Records for Schaumburg-Lippe (Berenbusch / Petzen, ca. 1725 – 1750)

While no complete census survives for this period, many *non-Kirchenbuch* sources can still identify every household in *Berenbusch* or nearby villages.  
They survive mainly in the *Niedersächsisches Landesarchiv Bückeburg (NLA BU)* under the *Amt Bückeburg* and *Rentkammer Bückeburg* series.

== 1. Record types most likely to help

[arabic]
. *Tax / Contribution Registers (Kontributions- & Schatzungsregister)*  
  Periodic lists of taxpayers by village and status (Colon, Anbauer, Einwohner).  
  Often note predecessors (“jetzt …, zuvor …”).  
  ➜ Show which *Krückeberg heads* paid in Berenbusch and their *status*—key for dividing children by household.  
  *Series:* mainly *K 1 Rentkammer Bückeburg* and *A 12 Amt Bückeburg*; also *F 2 „Kontributionen und Steuern“* and older *L 1 / K 90* fiscal fonds.

. *Land / Farm Registers (Lager-, Hof- or Meierbücher)*  
  Estate books listing each holding, acreage, dues, and tenant succession.  
  ➜ Tie a *named Krückeberg* to a specific farm; later entries back-reference earlier tenants.

. *Farm Charters & Estate Files (Hof-, Kauf-, Tausch-, Übergabe-, Meier-, Altenteil-, Inventar- & Vormundschaftsakten)*  
  Contracts when property changes hands or elders cede a farm.  
  ➜ Identify *fathers, sons, spouses,* and *house succession*; guardianships and inventories list *children with ages*.  
  *Series:* *K 1 Rentkammer Bückeburg*, *A 12 Amt Bückeburg*, *H 121d Freiwillige Gerichtsbarkeit* (post-death and guardianship matters).

. *Militia / Service Lists (Miliz-, Musterungs-, Dienstregister)*  
  Eligible males by village and year.  
  ➜ Confirm *which Krückeberg men* were alive in particular years.  
  *Series:* *F 2 Allgemeine Militärsachen*.

. *Fire-Insurance Registers (Brandkasse)*  
  House-by-house lists with owner/tenant, valuation, and often notes such as “jetzt …, vor diesem …”.  
  ➜ Useful for linking later and earlier occupants; typically late 18th c. but help extend continuity backward.  
  *Series:* Rentkammer or Amt Bückeburg.

. *Local Administrative / Forst / Boundary Files*  
  Routine *Untertanen-*, *Forst-*, or *Grenz-* cases.  
  ➜ Can place two Krückeberg men together as neighbors or heirs.

== 2. Coverage reality (1728 – 1750)

* Full enumerations do **not** exist that early.  
* What does exist are:
  * tax / land registers keyed to *heads of household*, and  
  * individual farm / inheritance / guardianship files when ownership changed.  
Together these can identify *each active Krückeberg household* in the village.

== 3. Where these records reside

|===
|Series / Fonds | Scope & contents | Why relevant

|*K 1 – Rentkammer Bückeburg* (_Älteres Kammerarchiv 1647 – 1777_)  
and *K 90 – Rechnungsregistratur (1615 – 1949)_
|Accounts, leases, tax lists by village
|Names of tenants and taxpayers for Berenbusch

|*A 12 – Amt Bückeburg*
|Local administration & lower court records
|Village-level files on labor service, militia, inheritance

|*F 2 – Allgemeine Militärsachen*
|Recruiting & militia rolls
|Lists of adult males by village and year

|*H 121d – Freiwillige Gerichtsbarkeit*
|Probate, inventories, guardianships
|Enumerates children, ages, spouses

|*L 2 – Alte Regierungsregistratur (1553 – 1823)*
|Earlier government files; transfers to Rentkammer ca. 1728
|Explains where fiscal files shifted in the period
|===

_All held at_ **Niedersächsisches Landesarchiv – Abteilung Bückeburg**, Schloßplatz 2, 31675 Bückeburg.  
Online catalogue: https://www.arcinsys.niedersachsen.de/

== 6. How to use findings

* Record for each hit:  
  `Year • Record type • Village • Status • Name • House/holding note • Kin • Predecessor/Successor`
* Map simultaneous *Krückeberg* heads and link them with *Vahlsing* or other witness families.  
* Correlate church baptisms with household data via *status* and *timeline*—e.g., “Colon Krückeberg” vs “Anbauer Krückeberg.”  
* Use later Brandkasse or lease records to project each holding backward into 1728 – 1750.

== 7. Why this matters for the Krückeberg reconstruction

These secular records can:

* show how many Krückeberg households co-existed in Berenbusch,  
* reveal who held which *Kolon* or farm,  
* document inheritance and property transfer, and  
* provide names where church books only say “Krückeberg in Berenbusch.”

Together they form the missing link between the sparse Petzen Kirchenbücher (1728 – 1750) and the detailed late-18th-century case files.
