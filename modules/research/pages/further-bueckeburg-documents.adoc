= Further Research Strategy

You are asking exactly the _right_ question now, and you are also
correct about the *limitations of baptism records* for proving residence
at a specific house number that early. The strategy therefore has to
shift away from _expecting_ "`Nr. 10`" in Kirchenbuch entries and toward
*triangulating residence indirectly*, using records that _do_ encode
continuity of place.

Below is a *methodologically sound, step-by-step strategy* tailored to
your situation.


== 1. First: reset expectations about Kirchenbuch evidence

=== Why baptisms won’t reliably solve this

Before ca. *1740–1750*, Petzen parish records:

* Rarely give *house numbers*
* Often give only:
** village name (_zu Berenbusch_)
** occupation
* Are often *faded* and *inconsistent*
* Do *not* distinguish between different Brinksitzer houses

So you are right:

____
Even if you find Eggerding or Kuhlmann baptisms, they may say only
"`Berenbusch`" — which does _not_ prove Nr. 10.
____

Therefore, baptism records alone *cannot answer* your question.


== 2. Reframe the core question precisely

What you really want to know is:

____
Was Johann Heinrich Krückeberg already *resident at the same Brinksitzer
place* (later Nr. 10) when he married in 1727, even if he was not yet
the recognized holder?
____

That is a *residence continuity question*, not a legal ownership
question.


== 3. The strongest strategy: work _backward_ from fixed points

You already have *three immovable anchors*:

[arabic]
. *1743–44*: Johann Heinrich is holder of Nr. 10
. *1745*: Same confirmed by Vermessung
. *1747*: Succession Eggerding → Krückeberg recorded

So the task is to determine whether *his residence predates his formal
recognition*.

This is common for Brinksitzer.


== 4. Key strategy #1: exploit _godparent networks_ (you already started this)

You have one crucial fact:

____
*Johann Tönnis Eggerding* was baptismal sponsor in *1728* for
Krückeberg’s son.
____

This is *strong circumstantial evidence* of:

* Physical proximity
* Ongoing social relationship
* Likely neighborhood or household adjacency

=== What to do next

Do _not_ stop at one baptism.

Systematically extract:

* All *baptisms 1727–1735* of Krückeberg children
* List *every sponsor*
* Identify:
** repeated names
** Berenbusch residents
** later-known Nr. 10 neighbors (from 1743–45)

If *the same small cluster* of Berenbusch names recurs, that strongly
suggests *stable residence*, not recent arrival.

This is one of the _few_ reliable pre-numbering residence indicators.


== 5. Key strategy #2: search for _negative evidence_ in 1737

You already know:

* Krückeberg, Kuhlmann, Eggerding are *absent* from the 1737 Hofbesitzer
list

But now ask a sharper question:

____
Are *any* Brinksitzer in Berenbusch listed in 1737?
____

If the answer is _no_, then:

* Absence proves *nothing about residence*
* Only that the list excludes Brinksitzer entirely

This supports the idea that Johann Heinrich could have been living at
the same place *for decades* without appearing.


== 6. Key strategy #3: Spezialvermessung (1734–1742) — but read it _structurally_

When you examine that document (you already have the identifier):

Do *not* look only for names.

Also note:

* Order of houses
* Relative position of Nr. 10
* Descriptions like:
** _Brink_
** _am Wege_
** _bei der Gemeinheit_
* Whether the same *plot description* later appears under Krückeberg

If the _plot description_ is stable while the _name changes_, that is
evidence of *continuous occupation*.


== 7. Key strategy #4: look for _craft-based residence stability_

Johann Heinrich is a *Schneider (tailor)*.

Tailors:

* Needed *local clientele*
* Rarely moved villages once established
* Often occupied the _same Brinksitzer place_ for decades

So if:

* He marries in 1727 "`aus Berenbusch`"
* Has children baptized there
* Is socially linked to Eggerding by 1728
* Later becomes holder of the same Brinksitzer place

Then the *most economical historical explanation* is:

____
He was already living at that Brinksitzer site before he formally
succeeded to it.
____

This is not speculation — it is *standard rural demographic behavior*.


== 8. What _not_ to over-invest in right now

* Trying to prove Eggerding ↔ Kuhlmann ↔ Krückeberg via baptisms alone
* Expecting house numbers before mid-18th century
* Relying on FamilySearch trees for this level of resolution

Those can help _context_, but not solve the problem.


== 9. What you can say _now_, defensibly

With your current evidence, you can already state:

____
Johann Heinrich Krückeberg lived in Berenbusch by 1727. He was socially
connected to the prior holder of Nr. 10 by 1728. He became the
recognized Brinksitzer of Nr. 10 by 1743–44. It is highly probable that
his residence at that site *predated his formal recognition*, although
the exact year cannot be documented from surviving records.
____

That is *sound historical reasoning*, not overreach.
