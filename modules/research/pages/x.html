Non-Church Records for Schaumburg-Lippe (Berenbusch / Petzen, ca. 1725–1750)

Although no full census survives for this period, non-Kirchenbuch records in the Niedersächsisches Landesarchiv – Abteilung Bückeburg (NLA BU) can still identify nearly every household in Berenbusch or nearby villages of the Amt Bückeburg.

1. Key record types

  1. Tax / Contribution Registers (Kontributions- & Schatzungsregister)

    These are periodic lists of taxpayers by village and status (Colon, Anbauer, Einwohner). They often note predecessors (“jetzt …, zuvor …”).

    ➜ They identify which Krückeberg heads paid in Berenbusch and their social rank.

  2. Land / Farm Registers (Lager-, Hof- or Meierbücher)

    These are lease books listing each holding, acreage, dues, and tenant succession.

    ➜ They link a named Krückeberg to a specific farm; later entries back-reference earlier tenants.

  3. Farm Charters & Estate Files (Hof-, Kauf-, Tausch-, Übergabe-, Meier- and Altenteilbriefe; Inventare & Vormundschaftsakten)

    These are contracts for transfers or retirements; probate and guardianship files.

    ➜ They identify parents, heirs, and spouses; inventories list children with ages.

    See Series:

    • K 1—​Rentkammer Bückeburg

    • A 12—​Amt Bückeburg

    • H 121d—​Freiwillige Gerichtsbarkeit.

  4. Militia / Service Lists (Miliz-, Musterungs-, Dienstregister)

    They enumerate eligible males by village and year.

    ➜ They fix Jobst vs Johann Heinrich Krückeberg to specific years.

    See Series:

    • F 2—​Allgemeine Militärsachen.

  5. Fire-Insurance Registers (Brandkasse)

    These are house-by-house valuations, often noting “jetzt …, vor diesem …”.

    ➜ They extend continuity between the 1790s and the 1720s.

    See Series: Rentkammer or Amt Bückeburg.

  6. Local Administrative / Forst / Boundary Files

    These are village-level disputes, services, or wood-use records.

    ➜ They would place two Krückeberg men in one context—brothers, heirs, or neighbors.

2. Coverage reality (1728–1750)

  • There are no true censuses; only household-head lists.

  • The surviving evidence includes:

    • tax / land registers keyed to household heads, and

    • inheritance / guardianship files when farms changed hands.

Combined, they can identify all Krückeberg households active in the village.

4. Step-by-step Arcinsys search strategy

Definition of "fonds"

Fonds (pronounced roughly fonz, from French) means:

The entire body of records that originate from a single creator — a person, family, or institution — accumulated naturally as the result of its activities and preserved as an organic whole.

In other words, a fonds is not an artificial collection arranged by subject, but the original aggregate of records produced by one entity in the course of doing its work.

🏛 In plain terms

Think of a fonds as:

“Everything that came out of one office, kept together because it all belonged to that office.”

For example:

Fonds Creator What it represents

K 1 Rentkammer Bückeburg

The Rentkammer (treasury/land office) of Schaumburg-Lippe

All land, tax, and lease files that office generated or received

A 12 Amt Bückeburg

The Amt Bückeburg (district administration)

All administrative, judicial, and police records created by that local government

H 121d Freiwillige Gerichtsbarkeit

The probate and guardianship courts

All wills, inventories, and guardianship files they maintained

Each of these is a fonds because it preserves the original context of record creation — who made the record and why.

🧩 Relationship to “series” and “files”

In most archival hierarchies (including the NLA’s Arcinsys):

Archive
 ├── Fonds (Bestand)
 │     ├── Series (Serie)
 │     │     └── Files (Akte, Band, Nr.)

  • The fonds = the whole record group from one office.

  • A series = a subdivision within that fonds (for example, “Tax rolls 1710–1800”).

  • An Akte or Nr. = a specific file or bound volume within that series.

When you search Arcinsys and see entries like K 1 Nr. 842 – Berenbusch, Kontributionsregister 1743–1752, you’re looking at:

  • Fonds: K 1 (Rentkammer Bückeburg)

  • Series: “Tax rolls” (implied)

  • File: Nr. 842 (the specific item you can request)

🧭 Why it matters

Understanding fonds helps you:

  • Keep context — know who produced a record and why.

  • Avoid mixing files from different creators (which can distort meaning).

  • Search more efficiently, since Arcinsys is organized by fonds rather than by topic.

What “series for Amt Bückeburg / Rentkammer … and Untergericht Bückeburg” means

The archive organizes its holdings (Bestände) by the government office that originally created them. For your period (1725–1750) the relevant offices for Berenbusch were:

  • Amt Bückeburg – the local administrative and judicial district that governed Petzen, Berenbusch, Nordholz, etc.

  • Rentkammer Bückeburg (also called Kammer- or Domänenverwaltung) – the treasury and land office that managed taxes, leases, and meierhof records.

  • Untergericht Bückeburg – the lower court that handled local legal and inheritance matters.

When you look in the NLA catalogue (Arcinsys), you search inside the series (Bestände) for those offices — that’s where documents about villagers actually appear.

What “Ortsregister / Findbücher keywords” means

Every archival series has an index (Findbuch or Ortsregister) — basically a searchable list of files. When you consult staff or use Arcinsys, you tell them the place and subject you’re after. For example, you would say:

Bitte nach Unterlagen im Bestand K 1 (Rentkammer Bückeburg) bzw. A 12 (Amt Bückeburg) mit den Stichwörtern Berenbusch, Petzen und den Sachbegriffen Kontribution, Schatzung, Lagerbuch, Meier, Hofbrief, Altenteil, Inventar, Vormundschaft, Miliz, Brandkasse suchen.

That means:

Please look in the Rentkammer and Amt Bückeburg series for files relating to Berenbusch/Petzen that concern taxation, land leases, farm charters, inheritance contracts, guardianships, militia lists, or fire-insurance records.

Search Strategy

  1. Limit the fonds. Start with Bestand = K 1 (Rentkammer Bückeburg).

  2. Run separate searches combining the place and subject:

  3. Repeat with Bestand = A 12 (Amt Bückeburg) adding Miliz, Musterung, Dienst.

  4. Check Laufzeiten. Favor files dated 1700–1750 (± 10 years). Later ones often reference earlier tenants.

  5. Record key data for each hit:

    Year • Record type • Village • Status • Name • Holding • Kin • Predecessor/Successor.

  6. Prioritize early Lagerbuch, Schatzregister/Kontribution, and Brandkasse for household lists; then Hofbrief/Altenteil/Inventar/Vormundschaft for kinship proof.

  7. Correlate with Petzen church entries by status and overlap (“Colon Krückeberg” vs “Anbauer Krückeberg”).

  • Determine how many Krückeberg households co-existed in Berenbusch.

  • Identify which held which Kolon (farm).

  • Trace inheritance and property succession.

  • Provide names where Petzen church books say only “Krückeberg in Berenbusch.” These records bridge the gap between the 1728–1750 Kirchenbücher and the later 1798–1800 case files.
