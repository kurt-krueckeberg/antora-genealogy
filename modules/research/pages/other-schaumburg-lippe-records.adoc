== Other *non-church* record groups for Petzen/Amt Bückeburg that can help sort Krückeberg

households in *Berenbusch (c. 1725–1750)*.

Below are the ones most likely to exist for *Schaumburg-Lippe* at NLA
Bückeburg (and why they help), in rough order of payoff.

=== Most promising (land/tax/household identity)

[arabic]
. *Kontributions- & Schatzungsregister (tax rolls)* +
Annual/periodic lists of taxpayers by village/Status (Colon,
Anbauer/Brinksitzer, Einwohner). Often note a head of household and sometimes
predecessors ("`jetzt …, zuvor …`"). +
➜ Lets you see *which Krückeberg heads* (Johann Heinrich, Jobst, etc.) paid in
Berenbusch and their *status*—perfect to split children by household.
. *Lager-/Hof-/Meier-Register (Rentkammer house & land books)* +
Estate/lease registers for each *Kolon/Meierhof*: tenant’s name,
obligations, acreages, transfers. +
➜ Ties a *named Krückeberg* to a *specific holding*; later entries often
back-reference earlier tenants (useful even if the exact year is a bit later
than 1750).
. *Hof-, Kauf-, Tausch-, Übergabe- & Meierbriefe (property/tenure
  charters)* Deeds/leases when a farm or part of it changed hands; include
  *giver/receiver, kin, witnesses*. +
➜ Identifies *fathers/sons* and the *house succession*, anchoring which adult
Krückeberg fathered which cohort.
. *Altenteil/Leibzucht contracts (retirement portion)* +
When an elder cedes the farm to the heir; lists *spouses, parents, children*, and support owed. +
➜ Names the *heir* and often the *spouse’s family* (Vahlsing links possible).

== Civil & court material (relationship clues)

[arabic, start=5]
. *Inventare & Teilungen / Testamente / Vormundschaftsakten* +

inventories and partitions; guardianship for minors. +
 ➜ Enumerate

. *Untergerichts-/Amtsprotokolle (local court minutes)* +

cases, acknowledgements, loans, sureties. +
 ➜ Put *two Krückeberg men* in the same entry with relationships (brother,
neighbor, son-in-law).

== Military & obligations (heads of household lists)

[arabic, start=7]
. *Landmiliz Muster- & Steuerrollen / Rekrutierungslisten* +
 Militia or
recruiting lists by village/status. +
 ➜ Name *eligible males* with
residence; good to see *Jobst vs. Johann Heinrich* alive in specific
years.
. *Frondienst-/Hand- & Spanndienst-Verzeichnisse* +
 Labor and draft-team
obligations (who turns up for road/estate work). +
 ➜ Confirms *which Krückeberg* held a team (often only the colon) vs. which were
smallholders.

=== Fiscal/administrative byplaces (sometimes earlier/later but useful)

[arabic, start=9]
. *Brandkasse (fire-insurance) registers* +
 _(often mid-18th c. +)_ House
lists with *owner/tenant*, construction details, valuations. +
 ➜ Even if a tad later, entries frequently say *"`jetzt …, vor diesem …`"*,
linking back to your timeframe.
. *Forst- und Holzlisten / Weide- & Hude-Register* +
 Rights to
wood/pasture allocated by household. +
 ➜ Another way to separate households in a village with few surnames.

=== Do Other Records Exist?

Yes—many *non-church* records for Schaumburg-Lippe *do* survive today,
and they’re held mainly at the *Niedersächsisches Landesarchiv,
Abteilung Bückeburg (NLA BU)*. The key is knowing which fonds to ask
for:

* *Hofkammer / Rentkammer records (finance/land):* _K 1 – Älteres
Kammerarchiv (1647–1777)_ and _K 90 – Rechnungsregistratur (1615–1949)_.
K 90’s description explicitly mentions long runs of *Amts-, Meierei-,
Mühlen-, Ziegelei- etc. accounts*—the kind of material that often
includes village-by-village payers and can reveal *heads of household*
in places like Berenbusch.
(https://www.arcinsys.niedersachsen.de/arcinsys/list.action?nodeid=g226&page=1&utm_source=chatgpt.com[arcinsys.niedersachsen.de])
* *Government central series (context & cross-refs):* _L 2 – Alte
Regierungsregistratur (1553–1823)_ notes that the *Rentkammer was spun
out ca. 1700/1728*, so fiscal/land files move from L-series into the
Kammer/Hofkammer series—useful background when you ask staff for
tax/lease books or lists.
(https://www.arcinsys.niedersachsen.de/arcinsys/detailAction?detailid=b659&utm_source=chatgpt.com[arcinsys.niedersachsen.de])
* *Military / militia (male lists):* _F 2 – Allgemeine Militärsachen_
includes items with *Aushebungslisten (recruiting rolls)* for
Schaumburg-Lippe; even if later examples are shown online, the fonds
itself spans the 18th–19th c. and is the right place to request earlier
rolls.
(https://www.arcinsys.niedersachsen.de/arcinsys/list.action?nodeid=g208261&page=1&utm_source=chatgpt.com[arcinsys.niedersachsen.de])
* *Probate/voluntary jurisdiction (heirs/guardians):* _H 121d –
Freiwillige Gerichtsbarkeit_ contains *testaments/inventories* and
related files (the series description explains it aggregates such
materials from mid-18th c. onward). These can name *children, spouses,
and ages*.
(https://www.arcinsys.niedersachsen.de/arcinsys/detailAction.action?detailid=b595&utm_source=chatgpt.com[arcinsys.niedersachsen.de])
* *Where and how to request:* NLA Bückeburg (Schloßplatz 2, 31675
Bückeburg) and its online catalog *Arcinsys* list these fonds and many
item-level entries. You can email the reading room and cite the fonds
codes above when asking for *Berenbusch* items (use place keywords like
_Berenbusch_, _Petzen_, _Amt Bückeburg_; record-type keywords like
_Kontribution_, _Meierei_, _Rechnungen_, _Inventar_, _Vormundschaft_).
(https://nla.niedersachsen.de/startseite/landesarchiv/abteilung_buckeburg/abteilung-buckeburg-193940.html?utm_source=chatgpt.com[nla.niedersachsen.de])

== How To Use NLA Bückeburg Archival Series Effectively

=== 1. What “series for Amt Bückeburg / Rentkammer … and Untergericht Bückeburg” means

The archive organizes its holdings (*Bestände*) by the government office that originally created them.  
For your period (1725–1750) the relevant offices for *Berenbusch* were:

* *Amt Bückeburg* – the local administrative and judicial district that governed Petzen, Berenbusch, Nordholz, etc.
* *Rentkammer Bückeburg* (also called *Kammer- or Domänenverwaltung*) – the treasury and land office that managed taxes, leases, and meierhof records.
* *Untergericht Bückeburg* – the lower court that handled local legal and inheritance matters.

When you look in the NLA catalogue (*Arcinsys*), you search inside the *series (Bestände)* for those offices — that’s where documents about villagers actually appear.

=== 2. What “Ortsregister / Findbücher keywords” means

Every archival series has an index (*Findbuch* or *Ortsregister*) — basically a searchable list of files.  
When you consult staff or use *Arcinsys*, you tell them the place and subject you’re after.  
For example, you would say:

> Bitte nach Unterlagen im Bestand K 1 (Rentkammer Bückeburg) bzw. A 12 (Amt Bückeburg) mit den Stichwörtern *Berenbusch*, *Petzen* und den Sachbegriffen *Kontribution*, *Schatzung*, *Lagerbuch*, *Meier*, *Hofbrief*, *Altenteil*, *Inventar*, *Vormundschaft*, *Miliz*, *Brandkasse* suchen.

That means:

> Please look in the Rentkammer and Amt Bückeburg series for files relating to Berenbusch/Petzen that concern taxation, land leases, farm charters, inheritance contracts, guardianships, militia lists, or fire-insurance records.

=== 3. What those German keywords stand for

|===
|Keyword | English meaning | What kind of info it yields

|*Kontribution / Schatzung*
|Tax assessments
|Lists of householders and payers

|*Lagerbuch / Meierregister*
|Rent or lease books
|Names of tenants and acreages

|*Hofbrief / Meierbrief / Kaufbrief*
|Farm or property charters
|Identifies buyer/seller, kin, and witnesses

|*Altenteil*
|Retirement portion / cession contract
|Lists parents and heirs; shows farm succession

|*Inventar / Vormundschaft*
|Probate or guardianship records
|Names of children, ages, and relationships

|*Militär / Milice*
|Militia or recruiting lists
|Adult males by village and year

|*Brandkasse*
|Fire-insurance registers
|Householders and building valuations
|===

These are the technical search words archivists recognize when filtering the indexes.

Berenbusch Lagerbuch

. Email the archive giving those references and ask whether the files cover 1725–1750 and can be viewed or copied.

=== 5. Why this matters for your research

Those series are the only surviving secular records that can show who lived in *Berenbusch* besides what the church books say.  
They can confirm:

* which *Krückeberg* households existed at the same time,
* which held which *Kolon* or farm,
* who inherited or sold land,
* who appears in *tax* or *militia* registers.

That is the paper trail that complements the Petzen *Kirchenbuch* entries.
