= One or Two Individuals
// One or Two Jobst Heinrichs?

== 1. One parish = one documentary universe

We are dealing with a *single Lutheran parish (Petzen)* that served:

* *Berenbusch*
* *Evesen*

That is crucial.

This means:

* *all Lutheran marriages* from both villages are in the same marriage
register,
* *all baptisms* of their children are in the same baptism register,
* *all confirmations* likewise.

There is no "`record boundary`" between Berenbusch and Evesen.

== 2. What two different men would necessarily produce

If there were *two distinct men* named _Jobst Heinrich Krückeberg_, both
Lutheran, both adult, both married, you would expect:

[arabic]
. *Two marriage entries*
* different brides
* possibly overlapping or parallel dates
. *Two sets of baptisms*
* children attributed to each man
* different maternal names
* possibly overlapping birth years
. *Two household trajectories*
* separate confirmation entries for children
* separate burial entries later

Church registers are unforgiving in this regard.

== 3. What you actually see

You see:

* *one marriage* in 1790
* *one wife*
* *one continuous series of baptisms*
* *one coherent occupational progression*
* *one death*

There is *no duplication*, no branching, no parallel family.

That alone is decisive.

== 4. Why administrative labels cannot override parish evidence

Administrative sources (Rentkammer, Amt):

* identify people by *status and location at a given moment*,
* may label by *current lodging* or *place of work*,
* are *not designed to distinguish family identity*.

Church registers *are* designed to do exactly that.

When there is a conflict:

____
*parish registers trump administrative shorthand for identity
questions*.
____

== 5. Why your conclusion is methodologically correct

Your reasoning follows best practice:

[arabic]
. Hypothesis: possibly two men
. Test: parish-wide marriage and baptism records
. Result: only one family
. Conclusion: one man with multiple residences/statuses

That is *proper genealogical proof logic*.

== Final answer (unambiguous)

Yes — you are correct.

Because:

* Evesen and Berenbusch belonged to the *same Petzen parish*,
* and because you see *only one marriage and one set of children*,
* there is compelling evidence that *only one Jobst Heinrich Krückeberg
existed* in this generation.

The different designations (_Schuster_, _Schneider_, _Einlieger in
Evesen_, _Hausherr in Berenbusch_) reflect *life circumstances*, not
different individuals.

The conclusion is sound and defensible.
